Wastewater and industrial water treatment
01Why now
2024/3019

EU Urban Wastewater Treatment Directive

Transposition by July 2027. Micropollutant (quaternary) treatment mandatory above 150,000 p.e.: 20% of plants by 2033, 60% by 2039, all of them by 2045.

≥ 80%

Paid by producers

Extended producer responsibility: the pharmaceutical and cosmetics industries fund at least 80% of quaternary treatment. New stages, new filters.

Frameworks

Spares are bought by tender

Thames Water runs a screen services framework covering all makes; in Portugal, SIMARSUL tenders screen spare parts brand by brand.

02The sums you already know
Today
The screen panel is ordered from the maker and arrives in weeks, if the model is still in the catalogue. The filter nozzle is from a brand that no longer exists. The strainer is a glued assembly that leaks where it should not, and stainless perforated plate is heavy, costly and needs tooling even for a small variant.
Sintered
The old part becomes a file with its revision, and the file becomes a batch: from one part to a few thousand, without a mould. Frame, clips, ribs and thread are born in the same part. The hole can widen towards the outlet so it does not hold, and next time the reorder is identical.
03Printed polypropylene is already in the plantPublic sources, at the foot of the page
Water utility, United Kingdom

United Utilities

In its Ofwat-funded Printfrastructure programme it uses printed polymer parts every day: jetting nozzles, instrument troughs and obsolete spares for filter arms.

Screen manufacturer

Johnson Screens

Moved the elements of the filtration belt of a wastewater inlet screen from stainless steel to printed polypropylene.

Water utility, United Kingdom

Anglian Water

The first part it trialled printing, with the University of Sheffield, was a filter nozzle.

Research, 2026

PP MBBR carriers

Gyroid biofilm carriers printed in PP, tested on domestic wastewater at pilot scale (Processes, 2026).

In production today

For an international water treatment group we produce, as a recurring series, sintered polypropylene filter panels with a 2 mm aperture: reordered from a digital warehouse, batch traced. Not a prototype: a supply.

Why polypropylene

It does not absorb water and does not hydrolyse, unlike polyamide. It withstands dilute acids and bases, caustic soda, ferric chloride and cleaning chemicals. It floats (about 0.9 g/cm³) and weighs about an eighth of stainless steel. It is what tanks, pipes and nozzles have always been made of.

Fig. 05 — Backwash strainer: body, slots and thread in one part. Right, section A-A.

  • Body, slots and thread in one part: no gluing, no joint to leak.

07The flip sideWhen it does not pay, we tell you first
  • Below 2 mm aperturethe fine slot of sand filtration stays with wedge wire and cloth.
  • Beyond 30 cmthe part is split into modules; pipes, sheets and large baffles are not our trade.
  • Drinking waterour PP holds no approval for water intended for human consumption.
  • Ozone and chlorine dioxidePP does not withstand them: that stage needs PVDF.
  • Sand and gritupstream of grit removal, polyurethane and UHMW last longer.
  • Pressure over timewe do not state a pressure rating: it is tested on your case.
  • Cold and sunbelow 0 °C PP becomes brittle on impact; outdoors, UV resistance is assessed.
